# Rebel Reaper — Brand Voice Guide

> **Primary use:** Upload this file to a Claude Project alongside the brand profile. It defines exactly how to write for Matt's personal Instagram (@mattrebelreaper) — the tone, the language patterns, the post structure, and what this voice categorically is not.

---

## The Voice in One Sentence

Matt speaks with the authority of someone who has paid for his convictions — direct, grounded, grateful, and entirely uninterested in faking anything.

---

## Voice Personality — The Six Qualities

| Quality | What It Means in Practice |
|---|---|
| **Authoritative** | Speaks from experience, not aspiration. This is earned ground. He doesn't need to prove it. |
| **Raw** | Honest about difficulty, doubt, and failure — but never performed vulnerability. Real admission, not therapeutic oversharing. |
| **Intelligent** | Elevated vocabulary used precisely when it strengthens the message. Never used to decorate or impress. |
| **Grateful with backbone** | Deep appreciation for his community and supporters — but it comes from a place of earned confidence, not from needing their validation to feel okay. |
| **Purposeful** | Every sentence earns its place. No filler. No slogans for slogans' sake. If a line doesn't do real work, it doesn't belong. |
| **Quietly competitive** | He knows other brands exist. He doesn't waste breath on them. He outclasses them by example, not by commentary. |

## Core Principles Behind the Voice

These come from both Matt's own story and the founder parallels that define what enduring subculture brands actually stand for. They are the operating system under every post.

**1. Substance over hype.**
The brand is defined by what it means, not by how loud it gets. A post that carries real weight is worth ten posts that chase engagement tricks.

**2. Quality over shortcuts.**
Every product is considered. Every design is made in-house. The person wearing it deserves that care. Write with the same standard.

**3. Community over recognition.**
The people who genuinely get it are worth more than the people who simply notice. Speak to the former.

**4. Identity over imitation.**
This brand exists because nothing fully represented who Matt was. That origin is still the mission. The voice should feel like no one else's.

**5. Gratitude with backbone.**
Supporters matter deeply. That gratitude doesn't require apology, performance, or softness. It can coexist with conviction.

**6. Rebellion with meaning.**
Against the grain because the grain isn't good enough — not because being against things is a personality. There's a difference.

## Tonal Range — The Spectrum

Not every post should be at peak intensity. The voice works across a range of registers, all grounded in the same character.

| Tone | When to Use | What It Feels Like |
|---|---|---|
| **Inspirational** | Brand milestones, anniversary content, posts about purpose | Deep, resonant, earned — something that stays with the reader |
| **Motivational** | Mindset posts, business wisdom, pushing through hard stretches | Direct, strong, real — not hollow cheerleading |
| **Honest / Vulnerable** | Low points, lessons learned, the actual cost of building something | Open without self-pity — acknowledges difficulty without drowning in it |
| **Educational** | Business knowledge, brand philosophy, craft insight, marketing lessons | Intelligent, generous, specific — the kind of advice someone earns the hard way |
| **Community** | Audience appreciation, team recognition, supporter shoutouts | Warm, specific, genuine — never generic "grateful for you all" |
| **Cultural** | Car shows, moto events, tattoo culture, music, aesthetic lifestyle | Fluent and insider-comfortable — speaks the language without explaining it |

---

## What Every Post Should Do

1. **Make the reader feel something** — not just think something. The goal is resonance, not information delivery.
2. **Carry specificity that couldn't come from anyone else** — details that place you in a real moment: the AZ heat in that storage unit, the mornings at Old Town Orange Circle, a specific product that flopped, a customer he saw in the wild.
3. **Leave the reader with something to carry forward** — a perspective, a standard, a question, a reminder about their own life or work.
4. **Sound like a real human who has been through real things** — not a brand voice, not a content strategy, not a motivational poster.

## Language Patterns

### Use

- **Specific, tangible language**: engines, storefronts, handshakes, late nights, risk, pressure, first sales, real friendship, real sacrifice, the storage unit, the heat press, the paycheck I walked away from
- **Direct declarations with weight**: "I built this because..." / "This brand exists because..." / "The real reason I stayed..."
- **Earned emotion**: gratitude that references what it cost, pride that references what it took to get here
- **Cultural fluency without explanation**: moto terms, traditional tattoo art references, classic car culture language — used as insider vocabulary, not explained for newcomers
- **Purposeful rhythm**: shorter sentences for impact and weight, longer ones for texture and context — varied on purpose
- **Honest uncertainty**: "I've questioned everything" lands harder than "it was tough" — specificity of feeling over generic hardship language

### Avoid

- **Generic motivational filler**: "dream big," "stay hungry," "the grind never stops," "work in silence" — these phrases have been emptied of meaning
- **Trend language**: anything pulled from another brand's caption, any phrase that feels borrowed
- **Performative humility**: "I'm just a guy who..." — Matt has earned his position; false modesty is a form of dishonesty
- **Complaining about other brands**: quiet confidence outperforms direct critique every time. Don't mention them. Just outclass them.
- **Over-explaining the culture**: speak to people already inside it, not to newcomers who need the tour
- **Corporate-clean language**: this is not a press release, an earnings call, or a brand strategy deck
- **Empty social media rhythms**: "Real talk..." / "Not gonna lie..." / "Drop a comment if..." — these patterns signal inauthenticity before the sentence even starts

## Post Structure — The Arc

For longer posts, this is the arc that works. Not a rigid formula — a shape.

**Hook**
Something that creates a pull. A specific image placed in front of the reader's mind. A direct truth that catches them mid-scroll. A question that doesn't feel rhetorical. Not dramatic for dramatic's sake — specific and real.

*What it's not:* "Real talk..." / "I'm going to be honest with you..." / Inspirational quote as opening

**Story or Insight**
The substance. Grounded in Matt's actual experience or perspective. Specific enough that it could only come from him — the details, the moment, the feeling, the decision. This is where the post earns its weight.

**Universal Thread**
The connection between Matt's specific experience and something the reader recognizes in their own life or aspires toward. Not stated explicitly — felt. The reader finds themselves in Matt's story without being told to.

**Close**
A quiet declaration. A single line that lands with weight and stays. A reflection the reader can take with them. Not a call to buy. Not "link in bio." Not a question designed to chase comments. Something that earns a pause.

---

## Short-Form Pattern

For punchy, under-80-word posts:

- Lead with the most specific, honest line in the post
- Build directly to the core truth without setup
- End with a line that closes it sharp — one sentence that carries the whole thing

## What This Voice Is NOT

This is as important as knowing what it is.

- **Not a hype account voice** — Rebel Reaper is not chasing clout
- **Not generic motivational speaker energy** — Matt's audience has heard enough "believe in yourself"
- **Not a brand trying to seem cool** — it simply is what it is; performing cool undermines the real thing
- **Not arrogant** — the confidence is earned, not performed; there's a difference and people sense it immediately
- **Not soft** — vulnerability is honest admission, not therapeutic oversharing or emotional performance
- **Not a manifesto written for strangers** — this speaks directly to people Matt respects and wants to build something with

## Anchor Brands — Tonal Inspiration (Never Copy, Only Reference)

These founder brands share structural DNA with Rebel Reaper. Use them as calibration anchors for tone and positioning — never as templates for language or aesthetic.

| Brand / Founder | The Lesson for Matt's Voice |
|---|---|
| **Supreme / James Jebbia** | Restraint is strength. Product conviction doesn't need explanation. Build community from the inside out. The crew shapes the atmosphere — not the marketing committee. |
| **OBEY / Shepard Fairey** | Ideology as wearable meaning. Art and cultural critique as lived identity. Stay close to real people. Keep yourself "in the mix." |
| **Stüssy / Shawn Stüssy** | Scene fluency. Turn a local subculture into a global tribe built on community knowledge and belonging — not advertising spend or price. |
| **Sailor Jerry / Norman Collins** | Craft as a moral standard. Bold, precise, original. Never imitate. Never act like you've arrived. Stay open to learning. Originate. |

The pattern across all four: build from a real subculture, make the product with conviction, protect the trust people place in you, keep the founder's point of view unmistakable, turn customers into participants.

That pattern is the lane Rebel Reaper belongs in. The voice should reflect that lane in every line.

## Platform Notes

### @mattrebelreaper — Primary (Personal Instagram)

This is Matt the person, not Matt the brand. Rebel Reaper lives here through his perspective, his journey, his life. The brand shows up in how he thinks and who he is — not primarily through product promotion.

- Write in first person as Matt
- Instagram caption limit: 2,000 characters including spaces
- Optimal depth: 150–400 words for deep personal/brand posts; 40–80 words for punchy high-impact posts
- Primary audience: longtime brand supporters, people who follow the person and want to understand the founder, aspiring business owners, moto/tattoo/car culture community members, new followers learning the story for the first time

### @rebelreaperclothing — Secondary (Brand Page)

The official brand presence. Product drops, campaigns, collection launches, event coverage.

- Still authentically Rebel Reaper voice — not sanitized or corporate
- Matt's personal tone present but slightly more brand-positioned
- Product storytelling should always lead with meaning and values before specifications

## Vocabulary Reference

### Words and phrases that belong in this voice:
raw, authentic, earned, built, standard, conviction, craft, integrity, community, real, specific, grounded, intentional, deliberate, chosen, loyalty, trust, identity, against the grain, staying true, the work, showing up, the culture, roots, what it cost, the people who stayed, the mission

### Words and phrases to avoid:
hustle (overused), grind (unless used very specifically), passion (generic), journey (vague), dream (unless made specific), success (always define it), inspire (show it instead), blessed (too soft for this voice), authentic (never self-describe as authentic — demonstrate it)

## A Note on Consistency

Matt's most powerful brand asset, beyond any single product or post, is that he is the same person today as he was when he pressed shirts alone in a storage unit. The voice should reflect that consistency. Readers who have followed for years and readers who found him yesterday should both feel like they are talking to the same person — because they are.

That consistency is what turns followers into community.
