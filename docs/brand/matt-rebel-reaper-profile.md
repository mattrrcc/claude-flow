## Who Matt Is

Matt is the founder and owner of Rebel Reaper, an independent apparel brand built at the convergence of traditional tattoo-inspired art, moto culture, classic car culture, hardcore music, and unapologetic individuality. He started the brand in 2016 after fifteen years in the mortgage industry — work that paid the bills and never fed the soul.

He is not a tattoo artist. The visual language of Rebel Reaper draws from the traditional tattoo art tradition — bold linework, flash iconography, Sailor Jerry-style composition and symbolism — but it is worn as a worldview, not a credential. Matt grew up surrounded by that aesthetic, and it is part of who he is in the same way a language is part of who you are when you grew up speaking it.

Matt is the same person today as he was on day one: direct, grounded, grateful for the people who have supported the brand, and completely unwilling to compromise what Rebel Reaper stands for to chase whatever is trending.

---

## The Formative Years

Matt grew up in California with deep roots in Orange County car culture. As a kid, he would wake up early and go to Old Town Orange Circle just to see the cars — hot rods, classics, engines that took years to build, people who cared about every bolt and painted surface. Those mornings shaped something durable in him: a belief that craft matters, that a thing should mean something, that the people who build it should love it.

He also grew up surrounded by the traditional tattoo art world. The bold aesthetics, the Sailor Jerry-inspired iconography, the art form's history of turning personal symbolism into something precise, permanent, and earned. He carries traditional tattoos over much of his body. The aesthetic is not a look he tried on — it is a part of his identity.

Layer in hardcore music, skate culture, band art, and the visual language of the underground, and you have the intersection of influences that eventually became Rebel Reaper's core aesthetic. These things were never separate for Matt. They were always pointing at the same thing: substance, craft, community, and the refusal to live like a copy of someone else.

In his twenties and into his thirties, Matt spent fifteen years in the mortgage industry. He was capable at the work. He was unfulfilled in it. The career never matched the person. He was always building something in the background — restless, looking for the work that actually fit who he was.

## Before Rebel Reaper

Before Rebel Reaper had a name, Matt tried to start three other clothing companies. None of them built traction. Not for lack of effort — for lack of identity. He was trying to build something before he had found the specific, honest vision that could hold it together. He recognized that. Took a deliberate break. Stepped back and spent real time sitting with who he actually was and what he genuinely wanted to put into the world.

What came back to him was specific and clear: there was no brand out there that represented the full intersection of his life — the car culture, the tattoo art aesthetic, the moto world, the music, the working-class creative pride, the individualism. Not all of it, together, honestly. So he built that brand himself.

Rebel Reaper was never a business idea in the conventional sense. It was a correction to a market that felt incomplete to him personally. That is exactly the kind of origin that produces a brand with staying power.

---

## The Founding — 2016

Rebel Reaper launched in 2016. From the start, it was not just a clothing company. It was a brand with a point of view — something that would look wrong to people who didn't understand it, and feel exactly right to the people who did.

The early days looked like this: Matt was still working his day job in the mortgage industry. After work, he drove to a 5x5 storage unit in the Arizona heat where his heat press lived. He pressed shirts, packed orders, shipped packages, and drove home. Then he woke up and did it again.

Two years in, the numbers started to move. Not quit-tomorrow money, but enough of a signal that something real was building. By 2018, he made the call — left the mortgage industry and went all-in on Rebel Reaper.

---

## The Grind

The early years of Rebel Reaper are a genuine founder's story. Small resources, big conviction, relentless action, and continuous uncertainty. Every product decision mattered because the margin for error was narrow. Every order that hit was earned. Every miss was painful and educational.

Those years included:

- The calculated risk of product orders — some that were absolute flops, some that became defining pieces and sold through fast
- Learning that hiring the right people is harder than building the product itself, and that the wrong hire costs more than money
- Physical storefronts: the overhead, the pressure, the conversations, the community those spaces created — and eventually the decision to step back from them to build the right foundation
- Marketing that never stays still — what worked last month is not what will work next month, and the willingness to learn and adapt is not optional, it is the job

There were moments he wanted to quit. Moments where everything felt fragile and misaligned. People came and went — some of them leaving real damage behind. Betrayal from people he trusted. The constant pressure of making decisions that affect other people's livelihoods while carrying the full creative and operational weight of the brand.

That pressure never fully disappears. The brands that last are run by people who learn to carry it without being crushed by it.

---

## 2020 — The Proof Point

When COVID hit, a lot of brands fell off. Some of them large. Some of them established. Four years into Rebel Reaper, Matt pushed through it. The brand didn't collapse. It didn't go quiet.

That survival wasn't just a business result — it became a permanent reference point in his internal framework. This thing is real. This can go further. The foundation holds even when everything external is trying to make it crack.

In the years since, Rebel Reaper built with more consistent momentum. Not overnight. Not viral. Steady and earned — the only kind that compounds into something that actually lasts.

## The Product Philosophy

Rebel Reaper expanded from the original heat press runs into leather and denim pieces, custom garments, T-shirts, hats, socks, and button-up shirts. The leather and denim work was especially important in the early growth period — it helped separate the brand when most independent apparel companies were living and dying on graphic tees alone.

Every design is made in-house by Matt or his core team. Nothing is handed off to a production line with a reference image and a purchase order. The creative work is the brand. The artwork is considered. The product development is intentional and unhurried.

Matt takes his time because the person wearing it deserves that. This is not a philosophical position for press releases — it is the actual standard he holds himself and his team to on every piece.

---

## The Team

Rebel Reaper has always run lean. Small by choice, not limitation. A tight circle of people Matt trusts, anchored by Joey — his right-hand man, who has built this alongside him and represents the kind of loyalty and investment that is rare and worth everything.

Matt is particular about who comes into the brand. He has hired the wrong people. He has had to fire people he believed in. He has learned from both experiences. The people who have stayed are the ones he actively wants to grow — not just for the brand, but as individuals.

He has no interest in surrounding himself with people who aren't driven, creative, and loyal. He has no time for negativity, drama, or people who treat the work as just another paycheck. That is not the standard, and it will never be.

## The Physical Storefronts

Matt has owned multiple physical retail locations. Those spaces created something a website cannot fully replicate — real community, regular customers who became part of the story, the energy of shared space and direct connection.

The decision to step back from physical retail and focus on online-first and exclusive product was not a failure. It was a read on what the brand actually needed to build the right foundation for the next phase. Taking a step back to take ten forward is real strategic thinking, not retreat.

A new physical location is part of the future. Matt misses the community that comes with having a physical presence. That version of the brand will exist again.

## Identity and Culture

Rebel Reaper is not a single-niche brand. It lives at the convergence of:

- **Traditional tattoo-inspired art** — bold, iconic, precise, Sailor Jerry-influenced visual language
- **Moto / motorcycle culture** — the community, the machines, the shared values of the riders
- **Classic car and hot rod culture** — the OC roots, the shows, the craft and community that live inside that world
- **Hardcore music and band art** — the texture of the aesthetic, the independent attitude
- **Skate culture** — the anti-establishment creative lineage
- **Alternative lifestyle and individuality** — for anyone who refuses to live like a copy of someone else

The moto community and the classic car community, Matt has come to understand, are built from the same people. The same values. The same welcoming texture. He grew up with hot rods before motorcycles entered his life, and when they did, the crossover felt natural — because it was.

Matt's relationship with the tattoo art community also runs deep. He collects relationships with tattoo artists. He draws inspiration from people whose work is built on craft, originality, and years of discipline. That directness of relationship between the art and the person making it is something he brings into the brand.

## The Founder Parallels That Sharpen This Story

Rebel Reaper's origin and philosophy share structural DNA with some of the most enduring founder-led brands in streetwear and subculture apparel:

**Supreme (James Jebbia)** began because Jebbia believed the real customer he knew — the serious older New York skater — wasn't being served by existing product. He made what he personally liked, refused to dumb it down, and built a store that became a community hangout because the crew itself shaped the atmosphere. Matt's founding instinct is the same: the brand didn't exist yet, so he built it.

**OBEY (Shepard Fairey)** defines itself at the intersection of art, cultural critique, and ideology as wearable meaning. Fairey described street art as a democratic medium that rewards talent and courage. Matt's brand carries a version of that same energy: the art is a vehicle for a worldview, not just a graphic on a blank.

**Stüssy (Shawn Stüssy)** started in Southern California subculture, grew without outside funding, and turned a local signature into a global tribe built on insider community knowledge — not price or advertising spend. Matt's roots in OC culture, his organic growth, and his community-first instincts follow the same pattern.

**Sailor Jerry (Norman Collins)** built his legacy on technical precision, bold simplicity, and an absolute refusal to imitate. His letters emphasize never acting like the top dog, always staying open to learning, and prioritizing origination over imitation. Matt's relationship with the traditional tattoo art tradition is filtered through that same ethic: honor the influence, make something original.

What these founders share — and what Matt shares with them — is this: build from a real subculture, make the product with conviction, protect the trust people place in the brand, keep the founder's point of view unmistakable, and turn customers into participants rather than anonymous transactions.

## The Current Chapter

Rebel Reaper is at a place where the trust has been built over time. The people who support the brand know what they are getting. The work is recognized. The standard is understood.

The focus now is deeper craft, more intentional product development, and expanding reach — including eventually new physical locations and broader international distribution. The direction is not trend-driven. It is mission-driven.

Matt stays present. He shows up consistently. He still personally thanks strangers he sees wearing Rebel Reaper out in the world — walks up, introduces himself, shakes hands, tells them he appreciates it. Every time. That has not changed.

## What Matt Wants His Audience to Carry

When people read his posts, watch his content, or follow his journey, Matt wants them to take something home. Not just a product. Not just a brand story. Something they can use.

He wants the person who is just starting out in business to understand what it actually takes. He wants the person who has been following the brand for years to feel like they know the founder as well as they know the brand. He wants the stranger who just discovered him to feel pulled in by something real.

The goal is never performance. The goal is authenticity that resonates so specifically that it feels universal.
