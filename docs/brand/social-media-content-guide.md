# Rebel Reaper — Social Media Content Guide

- **Short-Form** (~40–75 words) — for punchy high-impact posts

## PILLAR 1: ORIGIN & PURPOSE
*Why Rebel Reaper exists. The problem it was built to solve. What was missing.*

---

### Post 1.1 — "There Was No Brand"

**Long-Form**

I grew up around things with a point of view. Hot rods built by people who cared about every detail. Traditional tattoo art that carried real meaning in every line. Music that wasn't trying to be liked — it was trying to say something true. I had all of these worlds I lived inside, and I kept looking for a brand that represented the full intersection of all of them together.

It didn't exist.

Not the way I needed it to. There were brands I respected. Brands I bought from. But none of them were all of it — the cars, the art, the moto culture, the music, the aesthetic I'd been surrounded by my whole life. Every brand I found represented a piece of who I was. None of them represented the whole thing.

So in 2016, I stopped looking and started building.

Rebel Reaper was never a business idea I had at a whiteboard. It was a response to a gap I felt personally, for years, before I had the clarity to name it and the guts to do something about it. I built the brand I needed to exist.

That's still the standard. Every design, every product, every decision — I ask the same question I asked from the beginning: does this represent something real? Not just real to the market. Real to me.

If the answer is yes, we make it. If it's not, we don't. That's always been the line.

**Short-Form**

I looked for years for a brand that represented all of it — the car culture, the tattoo art, the moto world, the music. The full intersection of who I actually am.

It didn't exist.

So I built it. That's still the standard every time we make anything.

---

### Post 1.2 — "After Three Attempts"

**Long-Form**

I tried to build something three times before Rebel Reaper had a name. Three different clothing companies. Three different directions. None of them worked.

Not because I wasn't working — I was. But because I hadn't yet found the identity that was sharp and honest enough to hold the whole thing together. I was building before I fully understood what I was building toward. And you can only run that far on effort before the lack of clarity catches up to you.

So I stopped. Took real time off. Sat back and actually thought about who I was, what I genuinely cared about, and what I wanted to put into the world. Not what I thought would sell. Not what I thought people wanted. What I believed in.

What came out of that time was Rebel Reaper. Not a brand concept. A point of view I'd been living my whole life that I'd never quite had the language for until I stopped long enough to find it.

Clarity costs you something. For me, it cost time — failed attempts, a slower start than I wanted. But what came out of that time was real. Real enough to spend the next ten years building.

Sometimes you have to stop moving long enough to understand what you're actually moving toward.

**Short-Form**

Three other companies before Rebel Reaper. All of them fell short.

Not from lack of work — from lack of clarity. I was building before I understood what I was building toward.

Took time off. Sat with it. Found the real thing.

Sometimes you have to stop moving to understand what you're actually moving toward.

## PILLAR 2: THE GRIND
*What it actually took. The unglamorous early years. The specific cost of building something real.*

---

### Post 2.1 — "The Storage Unit"

**Long-Form**

When people find out I was pressing shirts in a 5x5 storage unit in the Arizona heat after my day job, the response is usually something like "that's inspiring." And I appreciate that. But what I remember most is that it was just the only option I had.

I wasn't waxing poetic about the hustle. I was pressing shirts because that's where the heat press fit. The storage unit because that's what I could afford. After work because that's the only time I had.

Night after night in that box in the heat, packaging orders, shipping them out, going home, and doing it again the next day.

That went on for two years. Day job during the day, Rebel Reaper at night. Until one day the numbers shifted enough that I could justify making the call.

I don't tell this story to make it sound harder than it was or to get credit for grinding. I tell it because it's the actual foundation. There's no version of this brand that exists without those two years in that storage unit. That's where the standard got set. That's where I learned that the work either means something to you or it doesn't, because there's nothing glamorous about it to fall back on. Just the work and whether you care enough to keep doing it.

I cared enough. I still do.

**Short-Form**

Two years. Day job during the day, storage unit in the Arizona heat at night. Heat press, orders, ship, repeat.

No glamour in it. No audience watching. Just the work and whether you care enough to keep showing up.

That's where the standard for this brand got set. Still holds today.

---

### Post 2.2 — "The Leap"

**Long-Form**

There's no version of going all-in that isn't terrifying. People talk about "taking the leap" like it's a clean moment — you step off the edge and the path appears. That's not how it felt.

2018. I had two years of after-work Rebel Reaper runs behind me. I had enough signal in the numbers to make a case for the jump. I had a 15-year career in mortgage I'd be walking away from — a career that paid well and would always pay, because the industry doesn't stop.

The math made sense. The fear didn't care about the math.

What I knew was that staying in that job while trying to build the brand part-time had a ceiling I could already see. The brand needed me fully in it. And if I was going to find out whether this was really something, I had to be willing to find out the hard way.

So I jumped. And I won't romanticize what came after — it was hard in ways I wasn't completely prepared for. But what I gained in those first full years wasn't just the brand growing. It was the understanding that I could build something real. That the conviction I had about what Rebel Reaper could be wasn't just enthusiasm. It was accurate.

Some things you can only learn by betting on yourself fully.

**Short-Form**

Walking away from a stable 15-year career to run this full-time was the scariest thing I've done.

The math made sense. The fear didn't care about the math.

Some things you can only learn by betting on yourself fully. I'm glad I did it.

## PILLAR 3: IDENTITY & CULTURE
*The moto, tattoo art, classic car, music worlds that built the brand — and the people inside them.*

---

### Post 3.1 — "Old Town Orange Circle"

**Long-Form**

When I was a kid in Orange County, my family would go to Old Town Orange Circle in the mornings to see the cars. Hot rods, classics, custom builds — engines that took years of someone's life to get right. The people standing next to them weren't just showing something off. They were showing you who they were.

That's what I understood about car culture before I had language for it. You could see it in the way someone talked about their build, the choices they made, the care they took. It wasn't performance. It was identity. Everything about the car told you something true about the person who built it.

I think about those mornings a lot when I'm making decisions about Rebel Reaper. The same instinct that made those builds mean something is the instinct I'm trying to hold onto with this brand. Make it with conviction. Make it personal. Make it right. Don't hand it off to someone who doesn't care.

The people who grew up inside car culture understand this immediately. So do the people inside the moto world. So do the people in the traditional tattoo community. The cultures are different but the values underneath them are identical: craft matters, standards exist, integrity shows.

That's the intersection this brand was built at.

**Short-Form**

Early mornings at Old Town Orange Circle watching hot rods and customs — that's one of the first places I understood that craft is identity. The choices you make in what you build tell people something true about you.

That instinct is still running everything we make at Rebel Reaper.

---

### Post 3.2 — "The Communities That Feel the Same"

**Long-Form**

I grew up with hot rods before I got into motorcycles. Different world, different machines, different vocabulary. But when I got into the moto community, I noticed immediately: these are the same people.

Same values. Same kind of welcoming energy toward someone new who genuinely respects what the culture is. Same depth of relationship that comes from shared experience on the road or at a show. Same immediate read on whether someone is real or performing.

I've come to believe that the classic car community and the motorcycle community draw from the same source. They look different on the surface — one group is standing next to a '56 Chevy, the other is on a chopper — but ask them both why they care and the answer lands in the same place. Something about the machines, the craftsmanship, the community, and the honest expression of who you are through what you choose to build or ride.

That's the intersection Rebel Reaper lives at. Not one or the other. Both, completely.

When I go to events, shows, and gatherings in either world, I'm not there as a vendor. I'm there because this is where I come from and who I am. The brand reflects that. It has to — because the people inside those communities see through anything that doesn't.

**Short-Form**

Hot rods before motorcycles. Different machines, same people.

The classic car community and the moto community draw from the same source. Same values, same welcome, same immediate read on whether someone's real.

That intersection is exactly where Rebel Reaper belongs. Always has been.

## PILLAR 4: BUSINESS WISDOM
*Lessons earned the hard way. For other owners and builders. What the years actually taught.*

---

### Post 4.1 — "Consistency Before Breakthrough"

**Long-Form**

We live in a world that has decided a brand should sell out every drop, go viral every post, and build an audience overnight. That's the story that gets told. It's also not the story of any brand I respect.

I've been doing this for ten years. Ten years of making product, putting it out, learning from what worked and what didn't, adjusting, and doing it again. There was no single moment where everything changed. There were a hundred small moments where I kept showing up when I didn't have a reason to except that I believed in what I was building.

Rebel Reaper survived COVID in 2020 when brands with way more resources couldn't. That wasn't luck. That was the compounding result of years of consistency and a customer base that trusted us because we had been honest with them from the beginning.

If you're building something and you're measuring yourself against brands that look like they exploded overnight, you're comparing your chapter one to someone else's chapter ten. Or you're comparing yourself to a highlight reel that omits the years of quiet, repetitive, unglamorous work that actually made it happen.

The work is what it is. Show up. Stay honest. Stay consistent. Trust the compounding effect of doing the right thing repeatedly over time.

It's slower than the overnight version. It's also the only version that lasts.

**Short-Form**

Ten years. No single breakthrough moment. Just consistent work, honest product, and a customer base that trusted us because we never gave them a reason not to.

That's what surviving COVID in 2020 when bigger brands didn't was actually about — the compounding result of showing up for years.

Consistency is the strategy. Nothing glamorous about it.

---

### Post 4.2 — "The People Around You"

**Long-Form**

Hiring and firing people is the hardest thing I've done as a business owner. Not because of the legal side or the operational side — but because of the human side.

When you hire someone, you're making a bet on who they are. When that bet is wrong, you don't just lose the work. You lose time, energy, trust, and sometimes pieces of the culture you've spent years building. I've had people come through this brand who I believed in completely, and who didn't earn that belief. That teaches you something about judgment, about reading people, about what to look for.

The people who have stayed — the ones who've been with me through multiple chapters of this — are worth more than any product decision I've ever made. They understand the mission not because I explained it to them, but because they feel it the way I feel it. That alignment is rare. When you find it, you protect it.

What I've learned is that the team is the brand. Not the designs. Not the products. The people who are building it every day. Small, tight, intentional. No room for people who don't actually care. No time for negativity or drama or people looking for a paycheck instead of a purpose.

You can't build something that means something with people who don't mean it.

**Short-Form**

Hiring and firing people is the hardest part of this. Not operationally — humanly.

The ones who've stayed understand the mission not because I explained it. Because they feel it.

The team is the brand. Small, tight, intentional. You can't build something that means something with people who don't mean it.

### Post 5.1 — "Still Shaking Hands"

**Long-Form**

Ten years in, and I still walk up to strangers when I see them wearing Rebel Reaper out in the world. I still thank them. Every time.

Some of them don't know what to make of it. They'll look down at their shirt like they're checking to make sure it's the right brand, and then look back up and I'm standing there. "That's my brand, man. I appreciate you."

I never want to lose that. It would be easy to get to a point where you've been doing this long enough that you start to feel entitled to the support instead of grateful for it. I've seen it happen with other people. I refuse to let it happen here.

Every person who puts on something with our name on it is making a decision with their body. They're saying this represents something about who I am. That's not nothing. That's one of the most real forms of endorsement that exists, and it doesn't require a deal or a post or an agreement.

The people who wear the brand — especially the ones who have been wearing it since the early years — are part of what this is. Not customers in the transactional sense. Part of the community in the real sense. That's what I'm always trying to honor.

**Short-Form**

Ten years in and I still walk up to strangers wearing the brand to shake their hand and thank them.

Every person who puts this on is making a decision with their body about what represents them. That's not nothing.

I never want to stop feeling that.

To the people who've been here from the beginning: I see you. I remember when you showed up. I know it meant something that you did, and I've tried to build something every year since that earns it.

## PILLAR 6: CRAFT & CREATIVE PROCESS
*What happens before the product exists. The design philosophy. Why in-house matters.*

---

### Post 6.1 — "Why We Make It In-House"

**Long-Form**

There are a thousand brands that will hand a concept to a production house and put their name on whatever comes back. I've watched it happen. I understand the efficiency argument. I also understand what you lose when you do it.

Every design that comes out of Rebel Reaper has been worked through by me or someone on my team. It doesn't get handed off. It doesn't get delegated to someone who has no personal relationship with what this brand means. The creative process is where the brand actually lives — long before the product is real.

That means some things take longer than they probably should. It means there are pieces we've sat on for months because they weren't right yet. It means I'm particular in ways that can be inconvenient. I'm not sorry about any of that.

The person wearing this deserves the care it takes. They're putting this on their body and they're saying something about who they are. That's a responsibility I take seriously. It's not just apparel. It's a piece of a worldview they're choosing to wear in public.

When I see brands that put their name on work they had no relationship with, I don't feel competitive. I feel the distance between what they're doing and what we're doing. It keeps my standards sharp.

**Short-Form**

Every design that leaves Rebel Reaper was worked through by me or my team. Nothing outsourced. Nothing handed off to someone without a personal relationship to what this brand means.

The person wearing it is putting it on their body and saying something about who they are. That deserves actual care. Not just production efficiency.

---

### Post 6.2 — "Leather and Denim Changed Things"

**Long-Form**

Early on, when the independent apparel world was full of brands living and dying on the strength of their graphic tees, we started doing leather and denim work. Custom pieces. Stuff that required more. That decision changed what conversations we were in.

It wasn't about being different for the sake of being different. It was about being honest about the culture we represent. Moto culture, traditional tattoo culture, classic car culture — these worlds have always had a relationship with leather and denim that goes beyond fashion. It's functional. It's earned. It's got history in it.

When we brought that into Rebel Reaper, it signaled something to the right people. It said this isn't a brand that's just drawing on subculture for aesthetics while staying safely in the lane of graphic tees. This is a brand that's actually trying to represent the culture it claims to represent.

That matters more than people might think. The communities we're part of — moto, tattoo, car culture — they have an immediate sense for what's real and what's borrowed. Leather and denim is not borrowed. It belongs here.

**Short-Form**

When other brands were surviving on graphic tees, we started doing leather and denim.

Not to be different. Because the culture we represent has always had a real relationship with those materials. Moto. Tattoo. Classic cars. Leather belongs there.

It said something to the right people. Still does.

## PILLAR 7: MINDSET
*Resilience, self-doubt, and what it takes to keep showing up. The internal work of ownership.*

---

### Post 7.1 — "The Times I Wanted to Quit"

**Long-Form**

I'm not going to pretend this has been a smooth ride or that the doubt has ever fully gone away.

There have been stretches where I questioned everything. Where I looked at the work and the risk and the people who had left and the product decisions that didn't land and I thought: maybe this isn't it. Maybe I misread the whole thing.

Those moments don't get talked about enough. The founder story gets told from the breakthrough backward — here's the success, here's what I overcame to get here. What it leaves out is the real-time experience of sitting inside the uncertainty when you don't know if there's a breakthrough coming.

What kept me going wasn't always conviction. Sometimes it was just stubbornness. The refusal to close the chapter without seeing it through. The understanding that the only thing worse than the risk of continuing was the certainty of stopping.

Over time, those low points did something useful: they made me better at the work. Better at reading people. Better at making decisions under pressure. Better at knowing what the brand actually stands for when I had to defend it internally, to myself, on a hard day.

I wouldn't be the owner or the person I am without the times I almost wasn't.

**Short-Form**

There have been stretches where I questioned everything. The work, the risk, whether I'd read the whole thing wrong.

The founder story gets told from the breakthrough backward. What it leaves out is what it actually feels like to sit inside uncertainty without knowing if the breakthrough is coming.

The hard periods built the owner I am now. I wouldn't trade them.

---

### Post 7.2 — "What Keeps You Moving"

**Long-Form**

You have to have a reason that doesn't depend on external validation, because external validation is inconsistent.

Some months, everything is moving. The product is landing, the community is engaged, and everything you're doing feels like it's adding up. Then you'll have a stretch where nothing seems to be working the way you expect it to, and the silence where you hoped for response.

If what keeps you going is only the good feedback, you won't survive the quiet stretches. You need something underneath it — a reason for the work that exists independently of whether anyone is clapping right now.

For me, it's always been that I believe in what this brand represents. I believe in the people inside these cultures — moto, tattoo art, car culture, music — the people who live by a standard and don't need to explain it to anyone. I'm building something for them. For myself. That purpose doesn't require external confirmation to stay real.

On the hard days, that's the thing I go back to. Not the metrics. Not what's trending. The reason I started. The reason I stayed.

If you're building something, find the version of that for yourself. It's the only thing that holds up when everything else gets unpredictable.

**Short-Form**

If what keeps you going is only the positive feedback, you won't survive the quiet stretches.

You need a reason for the work that doesn't require external confirmation to stay real.

For me, it's always been the belief in what this represents and who I'm building it for. That doesn't change with the metrics.

## PILLAR 8: BEHIND THE BRAND
*What people don't see. The full weight of independent brand ownership.*

---

### Post 8.1 — "Every Hat"

**Long-Form**

On any given day, I'm the creative director, the marketing department, the product development team, the customer service line, the operations manager, and whatever else needs to be handled. That's independent brand ownership. You wear everything.

People see the product and the posts and the events. They don't see the hours that go into the decision behind each product that nobody will ever know was that hard. The marketing shift that requires learning a completely different skill set than the one that worked six months ago. The vendor negotiation. The fulfillment problem. The product that came back wrong and had to be dealt with.

I'm not complaining about any of it. I chose this, every day, and I'd choose it again. But I say it because I think there's a version of "being a brand owner" that looks from the outside like an identity and feels from the inside like a discipline. It's craft, logistics, finance, relationships, community, creativity, and resilience all running at the same time.

The thing that makes it worth it isn't one big moment. It's the accumulation of choosing to build something real, over and over, when it would have been easier to choose something simpler.

I don't know another way to do it.

**Short-Form**

Creative director, marketing department, product development, operations, customer service — often all in the same day.

Independent brand ownership is a discipline, not just an identity. It's every hat at once.

The thing that makes it worth it is choosing to build something real, over and over, when easier options exist.

---

### Post 8.2 — "Why We Stepped Back From Storefronts"

**Long-Form**

I've had physical retail locations. Multiple of them. And I miss what those spaces created — the community, the regulars who became part of the story, the energy of real people in a real space engaging with the brand directly. That's something a website can't fully replace.

The decision to step back from physical retail wasn't a defeat. It was a read on what the brand needed in order to build the right foundation for where we're actually going. The overhead of retail, the staffing, the operational weight of maintaining multiple locations — it was consuming resources that needed to go into building something more sustainable and more intentional.

Sometimes you take a step back to take ten forward. That's not a motivational saying I'm applying to my situation. It's what actually happened. The years since stepping back have been spent building a foundation that a new physical presence can be anchored to properly — when the time is right and the build is right.

A new location is coming. When it does, it won't be a placeholder or a test. It will be built the same way everything in this brand gets built: with intention and at the right time.

**Short-Form**

Stepping back from physical storefronts wasn't a defeat. It was understanding that the overhead was consuming resources that needed to go toward building the right foundation.

Sometimes you take a step back to take ten forward.

A new location is part of the future. When it opens, it'll be built the right way.

## PILLAR 9: MILESTONES & PROOF POINTS

### Post 9.1 — "2020 — What It Proved"

**Long-Form**

2020 tested every brand. Some of the brands that fell during COVID were large — established names with resources I could only dream about at the time. They couldn't hold it together. Rebel Reaper pushed through.

Four years in when the pandemic hit. We weren't a massive operation. We didn't have the financial runway that bigger players had. What we had was a customer base that trusted us because we'd been honest and consistent with them for four years, and a foundation that held because we hadn't overextended in ways we couldn't absorb.

When we came out the other side, something had shifted internally for me. Not the confidence to relax — the confidence to keep building. The evidence was now there, in the most stressful test the market could produce: this brand is real. This can go further. The foundation holds.

I'm not crediting myself with something I should credit the people who kept buying and the team that kept working. But I'll say this — the years before 2020, every decision made with the long term in mind, every choice to stay true rather than chase, was preparation for a test I didn't know was coming. And it worked.

That's what playing the long game actually looks like.

**Short-Form**

When COVID hit in 2020, brands with way more resources than us fell off. Rebel Reaper pushed through.

Four years in. Customer trust built through consistency. A foundation that held because we hadn't overextended.

What that moment proved: this is real. The long game works.

The distance between that 5x5 storage unit in Arizona and where we are now is hard to put into a single post. A lot of years. A lot of decisions. A lot of things that worked and things that didn't.

Ten years is long enough that I can look back at who I was when this started and see the version of this brand owner who had no idea what was ahead. The risk. The growth. The people who came and went. The moments that tested whether I actually meant what I said the brand was about.

Evidence that the standards you held when nobody was watching were the exact standards that made the difference when people were. Evidence that authenticity — not as a marketing value but as a daily practice — compounds into something real over time.

The brand doesn't look the same as it did. I don't look the same. That's what growth means. But the line from who I was then to who I am now runs straight and unbroken.

Same person. Same mission. Ten years deeper.

**Short-Form**

Ten years. The storage unit to now.

The brand doesn't look the same. I don't look the same. That's what growth means.

But the line from who I was then to who I am now runs straight. Same person. Same mission. Just ten years deeper into understanding what it takes and why it matters.

## PILLAR 10: THE VISION
*Staying true while building forward. The next chapter without overpromising.*

---

### Post 10.1 — "Against the Trend"

**Long-Form**

The pressure to chase trends is real and it never fully goes away. There's always something happening in the market — something blowing up, something getting copied, something that looks like the thing you should be pivoting toward. And there's a version of brand ownership where you follow that pressure. Where you adjust to whatever has momentum this quarter and tell yourself it's evolution.

I made a decision early on that Rebel Reaper would be something different. That we would stay true to what this brand actually represents even when the trend pointed somewhere else. Not out of stubbornness — out of the understanding that the brands that actually last are the ones that know who they are and don't compromise it for short-term attention.

The communities we come from — moto, traditional tattoo art, classic car culture — they have a way of filtering out what's real and what's borrowed. They can see through a brand that claims their culture without living it. We live it. We always have. That's not something I say for positioning. It's something I say because it's been true since 2016 and it has to stay true or none of the rest of it matters.

Staying the course when the trends are pulling in a different direction is one of the hardest things in this business. It's also one of the most important.

**Short-Form**

There's always something trending that looks like what you should be pivoting toward.

Rebel Reaper made a decision early: stay true to what this actually represents, even when the market pressure points somewhere else.

The cultures we come from can see through a brand that borrows without living it. We live it. That's not going to change.

---

### Post 10.2 — "What We're Building Toward"

**Long-Form**

I'm not someone who maps out the next five years and sticks it on a wall. I stay present, make good decisions consistently, and trust that the right next move usually becomes clear when you're doing the work.

What I know is this: Rebel Reaper is at a place now where the trust is built, the standard is established, and the community around it is real. The foundation is solid enough that we can start building the next floor with confidence instead of hope.

That means more intentional product — fewer pieces made with more meaning. It means getting back to having a physical presence at some point, a space where the community can exist in person again. It means expanding the reach of the brand beyond where it is right now, into markets that haven't found us yet but will recognize us immediately when they do.

None of that changes what the brand is. The mission doesn't shift with growth. If anything, growth is only valuable if it deepens what we're actually about — not dilutes it. Every decision about where we go next gets measured against the same question I've been asking since the beginning: does this represent something real?

If it does, we build toward it.

**Short-Form**

More intentional product. A physical presence again. Growth that reaches people who haven't found us yet but will recognize us immediately when they do.

None of that changes what this brand is.

Every decision about where we go next gets measured against the same question we've always asked: does this represent something real?

If it does, we build toward it.

## Engagement Frameworks

### Story Slide Sequences

Use these structures for Instagram Stories — one slide per beat.

**The Origin Hook (3 slides)**
1. "The brand I was looking for didn't exist. So I built it." (bold text over image)
2. Brief visual of the culture/aesthetic intersection (moto + tattoo art + car culture imagery)
3. "This is what Rebel Reaper is. And why it exists." + link or swipe up

**The Real Talk (3 slides)**
1. "Something I don't talk about enough:" (text on dark background)
2. Honest admission — a low point, a lesson, a real moment from the journey
3. What came out of it / the takeaway
